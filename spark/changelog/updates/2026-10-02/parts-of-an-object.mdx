Feature · Web · Released [2026-10-02](/spark/changelog/changelog#2026-10-02)

An imported USD object is often made of many parts, such as a character, a prop it holds, or one mesh of a vehicle. You can select one part and hide, move, rotate, or resize it without changing the rest of the object.

You can also parent a camera to one part, so the camera follows a single character or vehicle inside a larger file. See [parenting a camera to a part](/spark/changelog/updates/2026-10-02/parent-a-camera-to-a-part).

## Selecting a part

1. Click the object in the 3D canvas.
2. Move the pointer over the object. The part under the pointer fills light orange shortly after the pointer stops.
3. Double-click the part. The part fills orange, and a path appears at the bottom of the canvas, such as `dinofight ▸ root ▸ Game_engine`. The path shows the object first and the part you selected last. In this example the object is `dinofight` and the part is the knight, which the file names `Game_engine`.
4. To reach a smaller piece, double-click again on the selected part. This selects the exact mesh under the pointer, such as the knight's sword.

The first double-click on a rigged character selects the whole character, not one piece of it.

### Moving between levels

A part can sit inside a larger part. Use the keyboard to move between these levels while a part is selected.

| Shortcut | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **Alt+Up** | Select the level above. From the top level, this selects the whole object. |
| **Alt+Down** | Go back down, as far as the part you double-clicked. |
| **Esc** | Step back one level: from the part to the whole object, then to nothing selected. |

While a part is selected, **Alt+Up** and **Alt+Down** do not zoom the timeline.

## The Parts row

In the Outliner, each imported object has a **Parts** row. The number on the row is how many parts the object contains. The row starts with nothing under it. A part gets its own row there once you select it.

The **Parts** row has two buttons:

- **Find a part** (the magnifier) opens a search box that lists every part in the object. Type to filter the list, then click a result to select that part.
- **Pick a part in the viewport** (the eyedropper) lets you click a part in the 3D canvas. This selects the exact mesh you click, not the whole character. Press **Esc** to cancel.

2. The parts in the object. A part marked **moves** has its own movement in the file.

The rows under **Parts** show what you changed:

- A dim row with a crossed-out eye is a part that is hidden in the active setup.
- A row marked **moved** is a part that is moved, rotated, or resized in the active setup.

## Hiding a part

1. Select the part.
2. Click the eye beside the path at the bottom of the canvas. Its tooltip is **Hide this mesh**.

The part disappears from the canvas, and its row under **Parts** turns dim. To show it again, click the same eye, which now reads **Show this mesh**, or press **Ctrl+Z**.

The part's row under **Parts** has an eye too. It does the same thing, and it is the way to show a hidden part that you can no longer click in the canvas.

## Transforming a part

You can move, rotate, and resize a part with the same transform gizmo you use for a whole object.

1. Select the part.
2. Choose **Move**, **Rotate**, or **Scale** with the buttons at the upper right of the 3D canvas. See [arranging objects](/spark/scene-editor/arranging-objects#transform-gizmo) for the shortcuts.
3. Drag the gizmo.

The gizmo is the only way to transform a part. While a part is selected, the properties panel still shows the **Position**, **Rotation**, and **Scale** of the whole object, not of the part.

Only that part changes. A message names the part and says it moved, and its row under **Parts** shows **moved**. The message and the row say **moved** for a rotation or a resize too. To put the part back, click **Reset transform** on its row.

<img
  src="/images/spark/updates/2026-10-02/part-moved.png"
  alt="The 3D scene after moving the knight with the gizmo in Move mode. The knight stands to the right of the dinosaur with the transform gizmo on it, and the Game_engine row under Parts is marked moved."
  style={{ borderRadius: '0.5rem' }}
/>

1. The part in its new place, with the gizmo on it. The rest of the object has not moved.
2. The part's row, marked **moved**.

<Note>
One mesh of a rigged character cannot be transformed by itself, because a skeleton drives its shape. The gizmo does not appear, and a message tells you to step up. Press **Alt+Up** until the whole character is selected, then transform it.
</Note>

## Putting a part back

To undo every change to a part at once, click the red trash button on its row under **Parts**. The button shows when the part is selected and the pointer is on its row. A window named **Remove Overrides** asks you to confirm. Click **Remove**.

<img
  src="/images/spark/updates/2026-10-02/part-remove-overrides.png"
  alt="The Remove Overrides window. It asks whether to remove overrides on Game_engine, says the mesh stays in the scene and returns to its original position and visibility, and has the buttons Cancel and Remove."
  style={{ borderRadius: '0.5rem' }}
/>

The part stays in the object. It returns to the position and visibility it has in the file, and its row leaves the **Parts** list. Press **Ctrl+Z** to bring the changes back.

<Warning>
**Remove Overrides** clears the part's changes in every setup, not only in the active one. To change one setup only, use the eye or **Reset transform** in that setup.
</Warning>

## Part changes and setups

Hiding or transforming a part applies to the active setup. A part hidden in **Setup A** is still visible in **Base Setup**. See [setups and cameras](/spark/scene-editor/setups-and-cameras) for how the active setup works.

A change you make in **Base Setup** also shows in the other setups, as long as a setup has no change of its own for that part. A part moved in **Base Setup** is marked **moved** in **Setup A** too.

**Clone this setup** copies part changes. A part that is hidden in the setup you clone is also hidden in the new setup.

Part changes are saved with the scene. Your teammates see them, and so does the Spark iOS app, within a few seconds. This is different from hiding a whole object with the eye on its Outliner row, which is saved only in your web browser. See [hiding an object in one setup](/spark/scene-editor/setups-and-cameras#hiding-an-object-in-one-setup).

## Parenting a camera to a part

A camera can follow one moving part instead of the whole object. You set this in the camera's **Parent** list, where each object's moving parts are listed under it. See [parenting a camera to a part](/spark/changelog/updates/2026-10-02/parent-a-camera-to-a-part).

## In the Spark iOS app

Parts of an imported object that you hide or move in the Spark web app look the same in the app. Each setup shows its own part changes, so switching setups in the **Setups** panel switches them too. A change made on the web reaches a scene that is open in the app within a few seconds.
